# Changelog

All notable changes to Mallard are listed here, newest first, in the format of
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Mallard uses
[semantic versioning](https://semver.org/): before 1.0, a minor release may
change results and inputs, and every such change is listed under **Changed**
with what to do about it. Numbers in parentheses are pull requests or issues.

Pull requests do not edit this file: each one that changes `src/` adds a
fragment to [`changelog/`](changelog/README.md), and a release folds the
fragments in (`python3 tools/dev/changelog.py release X.Y.Z`). Unreleased changes
are this section plus the fragments (`python3 tools/dev/changelog.py notes
Unreleased`); release notes start from `python3 tools/dev/changelog.py notes X.Y.Z`.

## [0.10.0] - 2026-11-13

### Added

- Every `MEAN_`, `COV_` and `PHASE_` field records what it averages in its snapshot block: `kind` (`reynolds`, `covariance`, `phase`), the variables it is formed `of`, the time `weighting`, its window `start` and `end`, `samples` and `interval`, and for phase means the bin, bin count, bin weight and period or trigger; their VTKHDF, HDF5 and XDMF datasets add `time: mean` to `cell_methods`. `mallard info` tabulates them and `tools/mallard/vtu.py` has `field_averages()` (#672).
- Boundary probes: the diffusive fluxes of the viscous flux, `SPECIES_DIFFUSIVE_FLUX_<species>` and `ENERGY_DIFFUSIVE_FLUX`, and the totals with the convective fluxes, `SPECIES_TOTAL_FLUX_<species>` and `ENERGY_TOTAL_FLUX`; the wall model's exchange-point state, `WALL_MODEL_U`, `WALL_MODEL_T` and `WALL_MODEL_H`; and a `moment` reduction (about `moment_center`) of vector variables such as the tractions (#591).
- The case view draws the case: `mallard check --view DIR` also writes `DIR/case-light.svg` and `DIR/case-dark.svg`, annotated images drawn by Mallard itself (2D: the plane with the mesh; 3D: an isometric view over three orthographic ones) with every boundary zone colored by its condition and labeled with its name, condition and values in their units, every region, probe and periodic pair labeled by name, a legend, axes, a scale bar and the domain's extent in its units. The ParaView file adds the mesh's cells, the probes on points, lines and surfaces of their own, each periodic pair with its faces' images, and draws curved boundary zones on their curved geometry; `mallard info MESH --view DIR` writes the view of a mesh alone (#546).
- `mallard check` without an input checks the environment, and `mallard check input.toml` ends with the same Environment section: the build and the solvers installed, Kokkos, every rank's host, device, CPUs and threads, MPI, GPU-aware MPI and NCCL (built, what the MPI library supports, whether the run uses them), HDF5 (serial or parallel), CGNS, zlib and KaMinPar, as text or `--format json`. Problems are diagnostics with their fix: `W-ENV-PLACEMENT` (the rank report's placement checks), `W-ENV-THREAD-BINDING`, `W-ENV-HOST-STAGING` and `E-ENV-GPU-AWARE-MPI`, which makes the exit status 1. Run it under `srun` or `mpirun` to check a job's ranks and nodes (#508).
- The run block's `build` gains `hdf5_parallel`: whether HDF5 was built with MPI (#508).
- `mallard check` breaks the memory down by category (solution and stages, faces and gradients, reconstruction tables, mesh, boundaries and models, chemistry, diagnostics, output staging, halo buffers, work memory, setup) at the setup's peak and in the steady state, per cell and in total, with the device memory outside Kokkos (CUDA's context and kernels' local memory) on GPUs (#550).
- `mallard check --memory 40GB` or `--memory a100-80` (also `a100-40`, `h100-80`, `h200`, `mi250x`, `mi300x`; by default the GPU's own memory in GPU builds) sizes the run: the most cells per GPU or rank, the fewest GPUs the mesh fits on with the largest part and its peak (the larger of the setup's peak and the steady state), and up to how many the halo stays below 10% of the owned cells. On an A100, from a check of 4 to 16 times fewer cells, the predicted peak is within -1.0% to +3.3% of the measured one. A run that does not fit the target is a warning (`W-SIZING-FIXED`, `W-SIZING-PARTS`) (#550).
- `mallard check` reports the resolution against the case's physical scales: the cell sizes; the Kolmogorov and Taylor lengths from the dissipation of the initial (or restart) field or of the `mass_flow` forcing; the friction velocity and the walls' first-cell y+, x+ and z+ from a restart's wall shear, Dean's channel correlation (`mass_flow`) or a flat plate's skin friction; the wall model's exchange points in wall units; cells across TFLES's `delta_L`. Each states its assumption, or why it is not estimated. Warnings, with the spacing to aim for, when DNS cells exceed 2 Kolmogorov lengths, first cells y+ 1, wall cells x+ 15 / z+ 8 (DNS) or 150 / 40 (wall-resolved LES), or a wall model's exchange point sits below y+ 30; `--quality` also writes their fields `Y_PLUS`, `DELTA_OVER_ETA` and `CELLS_PER_DELTA_L` (#548).
- `mallard check --format json` prints the same report as one JSON object (`version` 1), for scripts and CI (#657).
- Shell completion: `mallard list keys` completes the sections and keys of the key catalog, and `--fields` (`mallard post extract`, `mallard diff`) the output fields of the field catalog, each item of the comma-separated list (#770, #785).
- Derived fields `P0`, `T0`, `H0` (stagnation pressure, temperature and enthalpy), `SOUND_SPEED`, `ENTROPY`, `DILATATION`, `HELICITY` (3D), `ENSTROPHY`, `STRAIN_RATE` and `CP` (with the new `[output] reference`), and for mixtures `MIXTURE_FRACTION` (Bilger), `PROGRESS_VARIABLE`, `FLAME_INDEX` (Takeno) and `SCALAR_DISSIPATION`, relative to the new `[output.mixture]` streams, species and progress definition; all from one catalog that `[[write_data]]`, `mallard post derive` and `mallard list fields` share (#511).
- `mallard post derive FILE... --fields LIST`: derived fields (and `VORTICITY`) of restart files and snapshots after the run, from the run's own reconstruction and gradients, bit for bit what `[[write_data]]` writes at those steps from restart files; `--reference` and `--mixture` give the input a run lacked (#511, #858).
- `[output] reference` is also the default `reference` of the wall probes' `CF` and `CP`; a probe's own replaces it (#511).
- Derived output fields for `[[write_data]]`: `MACH` (with each cell's frozen gamma for mixtures), `Q_CRITERION`, `LAMBDA2` (the middle eigenvalue of S² + Ω², by Jacobi rotations on the device; in 2D, of the flow taken as 3D with no z variation, with the hoop strain in axisymmetric runs) and `SCHLIEREN` (|∇ρ|), computed from the cell gradients of the state (part of #511).
- `mallard diff` prints its report as `mallard check` does: a status column and section headings that read `ok` (identical), `WARN` (within tolerance) or `ERROR` (beyond it), units in each table's units column, a row per component of a vector among the largest differences, and the result with its exit status on the first and last lines. `--format json` names the statuses as `mallard check --format json` does: `ok`, `warning`, `error`.
- A run that diverges diagnoses the failure before it stops: the divergence report names the invalid cells (global ids, ranks, what failed first), the cell closest to failing and its neighbors in the last valid state (state, local CFL number, mesh quality, nearest boundary, repairs, TENO and limiter activity, chemistry sub-steps) and the last progress rows, and ranks the likely causes (time step, poor cells, a boundary condition, the initial state, chemistry, the scheme) with their evidence and what to try. It is printed, written to `divergence_report.txt` and `.json` (`[output] divergence_report`), added to the run record as a `divergence` record, and comes with a snapshot of the failure's neighborhood for ParaView; the error message names the most likely cause. The report is in sections and tables with units: what and where, the likely causes with their weights, the cells of the evidence (density, speed, pressure, temperature, CFL number, quality, boundary, activity), the progress rows before the failure; its JSON gives the units of its values. Nothing is gathered while the run is healthy (#549, #659).
- `[les] gradients = "reconstruction"` (TENO only, experimental, off by default): the subgrid models take the velocity gradient of TENO's central polynomial in its smooth cells instead of the second-order least-squares gradient, which halves the eddy viscosity on coarse meshes. Decaying isotropic turbulence with TENO5 and HLLC: spectral error 0.112 / 0.113 -> 0.091 / 0.094 at 32^3, but 0.117 / 0.140 -> 0.120 / 0.156 at 64^3; about 7% more time per step (#660).
- Steps decided on the device and enqueued ahead of the GPU (`[run] step_queue`, 3 steps by default), so CUDA graph runs launch the next step without waiting for the previous one. The device computes dt, t, the step's end, error-control retries, the NaN check, p_max, the conservation ledger and each progress row's values (with the run health and the range check of boundary values of `t`), the force and integral monitors (with the kinetic-energy budget), probes written by step or by time and the samples of time-averaged probes, the `[statistics]` means and batches, spectra, phase averages and conditional statistics (with the derived fields, `HRR` and `OMEGA_<species>` they read), and evaluates `[[triggers]]`, `wall` included; their reductions over the ranks use NCCL in NCCL builds. The device stops for the host only at a step it must act on: an output, a probe reading `[statistics]` outputs, a trigger that fires, a row of a `[statistics.convergence]` signal that a trigger reads, a checkpoint, a request (a signal, `mallard ctl`, a control file, the wall time), the end of the run or a divergence. Results and files are the same bit for bit at any depth. Steps ahead of the host need the conditional nodes of CUDA graphs (CUDA 12.4 or later); runs with triggers that read a probe's value, and the progress rows, monitors, means and probes of reacting runs with fused half steps, take one step at a time (#730, #906, #914).
- `direction` of inlets and moving walls may have expressions of `x`, `y`, `z`, `t` among its components (a swirl, a direction that turns in time): normalized at each face, evaluated per stage on the device when it depends on `t`, and at inlets checked to point into the domain, at setup and at every stage, with the face, time and value.
- Synthetic turbulence on an `inlet_state` whose `u` depends on `t`: the fluctuations ride on the mean evaluated on the device at every stage, with the statistics of the fluctuations unchanged; `convection_velocity` defaults to the mean inflow speed at `t = 0`. A `reynolds_stress` of `t`, or a `u` of `t` together with `turbulence.profile`, is an error naming the key to change (#600).
- The run block records every file the run read besides its input and mesh, as `files`: mechanisms, CSV tables, inflow profiles and probe point files with their SHA-256; restart files and remap sources by the run, step and time that wrote them and the SHA-256 of that run's output record, without hashing them again. `mallard info` lists them, and `mallard diff` notes input files that differ between the runs of A and B (#668).
- Probes of isosurfaces, and of other surfaces and curves of the solution, take `average = true`: each sample extracts the surface anew, and the time averages are kept for each cell it has crossed, also in restart files, each cell weighted by the mean area of the surface in it, so that the averaged integral is the mean of the integrals and the averaged mean is that integral over the mean area (#798, #836).
- `[numerics.hybrid] central` selects the states of the hybrid flux's central part: `cell_averages`, KEEP on the two cells' values (with each cell's `gamma` and `e0` for gas mixtures, double flux included), which changes the kinetic energy by exactly the pressure work on interior faces, or `reconstructed`, KEEP on the reconstructed face states (#689, #801).
- `mallard list fields` lists every output field with its units, description, what a case needs to have it, and where it can be had: written by `[[write_data]]`, sampled by `[[probes]]`, averaged by `[statistics]` or derived after the run by `mallard post derive` (#502). `mallard list fields input.toml` reads the input and its mechanism, not the mesh, which need not exist yet, and lists exactly the fields its `[[write_data]] fields` accept, then the common ones it lacks and why (`MU_T: needs [les]`) (#786). `mallard list species input.toml` lists a case's species, and `mallard list templates` the case templates. The text is a table per group, with the units of every field (`dimensionless` where it has none); `list fields` and `list species` take `--format json` for scripts.
- `mallard list keys [SECTION] [INPUT]`: the key catalog as tables, by input table, with each key's type, units, default and accepted values; with an input file, the keys that case's setup reads and whether the input sets each (what `mallard check --catalog` writes); `--format json` for scripts (#502).
- Each rank, and a serial run, can number its cells along a Hilbert curve for memory locality, so a mesh file's numbering no longer sets the speed of a run (a file numbered without locality was 2-6x slower on one GPU); results and output files are unchanged. `[mesh] cell_order = "auto"`, the default, counts the cache lines a block of cells reads over the reconstruction's stencil in both orders and takes the curve when it reads a tenth fewer (on an A100: a Gmsh sphere mesh 28% faster, TENO on generated boxes 3-7% and MUSCL in 2D 3%, while MUSCL on generated 3D boxes keeps their order); the run log's Mesh section says which. `"hilbert"` and `"input"` force either. New benchmark `sphere_3d` on a Gmsh mesh (#478, #277).
- `mallard -i input.toml --log-format jsonl` prints the run record's progress records on stdout, one JSON object per line at each progress row, for live monitoring and plotting, also with `[output] run_log` off; the text goes to stderr (#827).
- `mallard diff A B` compares two snapshot, restart, probe or CSV files, of any format each: per field the values that differ bit for bit, the L1, L2 and Linf norms of B - A in the field's units and relative to A, and the cell (with its coordinates) and values of the largest difference; for restart files also the step, time, attributes, arrays and face records, bit for bit. `--tol` sets a relative tolerance per field and the exit status (0 within it, 1 beyond, 3 not comparable) makes it a regression check for scripts and CI; `--remap` compares files on different meshes through the conservative remap; `--format json` prints it for scripts.
- `mallard pack RUN_DIR` writes one archive that reproduces a run, from its run record: the input as the run read it, the resolved input, the mesh file and every other file it read (each checked against the SHA-256 the run recorded), its start record and run record, and its small outputs (forces, integrals, probes; `--with-outputs all` adds snapshots and restart files), with a `manifest.json` of each file's size and SHA-256 and the command that reruns it. The archive is tar, gzip-compressed in builds with zlib. `mallard pack --verify ARCHIVE` checks every file against the manifest and the run block (#519).
- `inlet_mass_flow`: `mdot` and `profile` may depend on `t`. They are evaluated on the device at every stage and the faces carry exactly `mdot(t)`, identically on any rank count; an `mdot` of `x`, `y`, `z` is an error that points to `profile` and `mass_flux`, and an `mdot` or profile that turns negative stops the run with the time and value (#601).
- Mesh and stencil fields (#521): `mallard check input.toml --mesh-fields PREFIX` writes the mesh-quality fields and the reconstruction's stencil fields `STENCIL_SIZE`, `STENCIL_SECTORS`, `STENCIL_DEGREE`, `STENCIL_CONDITION` (condition number of the central least-squares fit of TENO, or of MUSCL's gradient fit), `STENCIL_WALL`, `STENCIL_SEAM` and `STENCIL_PARTITION` in one file; `--cells IDS` or `--region NAME|TABLE` adds a `STENCIL_<id>` field per selected cell, and with `--stencils all`, `--cells` or `--region` it writes `PREFIX_stencils.h5`, each cell's stencils as the run builds them (members by global id with their stencil, mirror or periodic role and boundary face, per-cell degree, sizes, condition and reach, the owning ranks, and with `--weights` TENO's pseudo-inverse rows or MUSCL's gradient weights, as the step's own fits compute them), the same file whatever the number of ranks. A run writes the fields through `[[write_data]]` `fields` and every cell's stencils with `[output] write_stencils`. `mallard info mesh.msh --mesh-fields PREFIX` writes the quality fields of a mesh file, `tools/mallard/stencils.py` reads the stencil file, and the TENO stencil figure of the guide is drawn from it.
- Every output file is written as `NAME.partial` and gets its name once complete, as restart files already were: snapshots (each rank's piece too), their `.pvd`, `.vtkhdf.series` and `.xmf` indexes, XDMF data and mesh files, probe snapshots and spectra. A file under its own name is never cut short by a killed job; `mallard info`, `mallard diff` and `mallard post extract` refuse a `.partial` file and say its write did not finish, and `mallard info DIR` lists it (#669).
- The `output` records of `mallard.jsonl` are written once a snapshot is complete, also when written in the background, with `complete`, `bytes` and every file of the snapshot (each rank's piece too) with its size and SHA-256, hashed by one thread off the main thread, on the CPU's SHA instructions where it has them (ARMv8 SHA2, x86 SHA-NI). A snapshot without a record was not completely written (#669).
- `mallard info DIR` checks every file the run log records against its record: a missing file, or another size, is reported and exits 1; `--verify` also compares each file's SHA-256 (#669).
- The run summary's Writing line gives the time spent hashing the files for the run log, and how long the run waited for it when hashing fell behind writing (#669).
- `mallard info FILE` reports what any file Mallard writes or reads holds, without an input file: kind, format version, mesh, times, fields with units and ranges, species and run block; for mesh files also their quality (`--mesh-fields PREFIX` writes it as fields). `mallard info RUN_DIR` lists a run's start, end and output files, `--input` prints the input that produced a file, and `--format json` prints any of it for scripts; the text is tables, with the units of every quantity in its column header or line. It reads every snapshot, series, restart, probe and CSV format, of any dimension and precision, through the one reader that the `mallard post` commands and the Python package share.
- `mallard post average --region` with a region defined by the solution also averages covariances (`--products`: `integral_MEAN_<a>_<b>`, `COV_<a>_<b>`, `RMS_<a>`), Favre averages (`--favre`: `FAVRE_MEAN_<a>`, `FAVRE_COV_<a>_<b>`) and profiles over homogeneous directions (`--homogeneous`, each cell weighted by the time average of its measure in the region), and takes booleans of such a region with regions of the mesh (#851).
- `mallard post average --region` takes regions defined by the solution (`where`, `isosurface`): each is placed on the state of every snapshot averaged, and the time averages of the integrals and means of the fields over it are written to `OUTPUT/NAME_reductions.csv`, as an averaged `[[probes]]` entry writes them (#799).
- `mallard post average FILE...`: means, RMS, Reynolds and Favre covariances over saved restart files and snapshots, per cell as `[statistics]` keeps them (by its own code, bit for bit from restart files of the steps a run sampled), or over homogeneous directions as a CSV profile (`--homogeneous x,z`, with the dispersive part), the same bit for bit on any number of ranks and any `[mesh] cell_order`; `--statistics` averages a run's `[statistics]` snapshot over homogeneous directions, exactly as the profile of its states; `--region` restricts it; one state in memory at a time (#512).
- `[statistics]` `fields` and `products` take the derived fields (`MACH`, `P0`, `ENSTROPHY`, `MIXTURE_FRACTION`, ...), computed at each sample as `[[write_data]]` writes them (#512).
- `mallard post convergence` studies the tables `mallard post extract` wrote into a run's directory from that run's snapshots, matched by the run id, step and time each snapshot records; the report names those tables and `--format json` marks their quantities `extracted` (#812).
- `mallard post extract` lists each snapshot it read in its run block's `files` (`kind` `snapshot`, with the `run_id`, `step` and `t` of the run that wrote it); a file read at several steps is listed once per step (#812).
- `mallard post convergence --field NAME` without `--exact`: the error norms of a field against the finest run, remapped conservatively onto each coarser run's cells, and their observed orders, for cases without an exact solution (#769).
- `mallard post convergence RUN_DIR...`: the observed order of accuracy, the Richardson-extrapolated value and the grid convergence index (Celik et al. 2008, Roache's factors of safety: 1.25 with three or more runs, 3 with two and an assumed order) of every force, integral and probe table of runs at several resolutions, identified by their run records (run ids, cell counts, inputs) rather than their names; each quantity's convergence behavior (monotonic, oscillatory, divergent) and a verdict in words, its last value, its value at `--time` or its mean over `--average`, and with `--field NAME --exact EXPR` the L1, L2 and Linf errors of a field against an exact solution with their observed orders; text or `--format json` (#514).
- `mallard post extract FILE...` writes probes of existing output without rerunning: the input's `[[probes]]` (`--probe` chooses them), a region or probe type given with `--region` (points, curves, cut planes, surfaces with fluxes, region integrals, means and extremes), and with `--snapshot` snapshots of chosen fields and regions, on restart files, VTKHDF and XDMF snapshots and their series. The case comes from the file's run block (its relative mesh file, mechanism and TENO `cache_file` resolved from the run's directory) or `-i`; `--steps`, `--times`, `--every` and `--latest` choose the snapshots; the run's own probe code writes the files, so restart files give the run's probe values bit for bit, on any number of ranks. The report lists the files written and the last reductions with their units, or `--format json`.
- Curve probes (`line`, `polyline`, `circle`, `arc`, `curve`) take `n_points = "cells"`: one sample per cell the curve crosses, cut exactly at the cells' faces (curved cells by their curved shapes), with three Gauss points per crossing, so line integrals are exact for the reconstruction polynomials (#592).
- Filled region probes take `sampling = "point"`: the reconstruction integrated over the region's own shape, with high-order quadrature over the cells its surface cuts fitted to a few points, exact for the reconstruction polynomials; and `n_points = [n_x, n_y(, n_z)]`, samples at the nodes of a grid inside the region, equal to `points` probes at those nodes (#592).
- Probe files record where each sample is read: a probe sampling block (`mallard-probe-sampling`, a `# mallard-probe-sampling` line of CSV files, the root attribute `mallard_sampling` of HDF5 and VTKHDF files, a comment of VTP files) holds the sampling mode, the reconstruction (method, order, fallbacks), each column's value kind and average, per point the requested location, owning cell, its centroid and the distance to where the value is read, and for clipped probes the samples dropped outside the mesh. `mallard info` prints it and `tools/mallard/vtu.py` has `probe_sampling()` (#673).
- The run record's progress records hold each probe's latest reductions and one-point values (`probes`, the first 64 over every probe), with their units in the start record; `report.html` plots them, a group per probe (#716).
- Probes compute time spectra during the run: `spectra = {fields, length, ...}` on a `[[probes]]` entry keeps a history of the samples (or reductions) and writes their one-sided power spectral density by Welch's method (Hann or rectangular window, segments overlapping by half) with units, at the end of the run and every `time_interval`, reports each spectrum's peak frequency and Strouhal number in the log and the run record, and is carried bitwise by restart files (#614).
- Probes read the `[statistics]` averages as variables (`MEAN_<A>`, `MEAN_U`, `COV_<A>_<B>`, `PHASE_<bin>_<A>`), each sample the value of its cell; and `average = true` writes the running time averages of a probe's own samples and reductions (`MEAN_<variable>`), for point values, fluxes and wall quantities, sampled on the `[statistics]` schedule, the same on any number of ranks and continued bitwise by restart files (#590).
- Every installer installs the Python package `mallard`: the container images have it in their `python3`, `spack load mallard` puts it on `PYTHONPATH` with Spack's Python and numpy (variant `python`, on by default; `~python` leaves it out), and source builds make its library by default (`Mallard_ENABLE_PYTHON` is on; `-DMallard_PYTHON_INSTALL_DIR` makes `cmake --install` copy the package into a `site-packages` directory). The smoke test of an install reads the Sod run with it (#791).
- The Python package `mallard` (`tools/mallard`, `pip install -e tools`): `mallard.open` reads every file Mallard writes or reads (VTKHDF, VTU, PVTU, XDMF output and their series indexes, region snapshots, restart files, probe CSV, HDF5 and VTKHDF files, forces, integrals and spectra tables, mesh files) into numpy arrays with their units, snapshots and run block, and `geometry()` their points and cells, through Mallard's own reader; `mallard.run_record` and `mallard.run_files` read a run directory; `mallard.info`, `mallard.diff`, `mallard.post.extract` and `mallard.post.convergence` run the commands and return their JSON. The reader is the library `libmallard2d`/`libmallard3d` of a build with `-DMallard_ENABLE_PYTHON=ON`; numpy is the only dependency. Guide: `docs/guide/python.md`; reference: `docs/guide/python_api.md` (#517).
- `[[statistics.conditional]]`: PDFs and joint PDFs of one or two variables (the mixture fraction, the progress variable, any `[statistics]` field) and conditional means and variances of fields on them, by volume or mass (Favre), optionally in a region, on the device; per-sample sums exact in fixed point, reduced over the ranks with NCCL or MPI, so the results are the same bit for bit on any number of ranks and across restarts. `mallard post average --conditional` computes them from saved snapshots, bit for bit the run's from restart files of its sampling steps (#513).
- `[statistics]` samples the heat release rate `HRR` and the production rates `OMEGA_<species>` of reacting runs, in means, covariances and conditional statistics (#513).
- Regions repeat: `repeat = {count, translate}` or `{count, rotate, pivot}`, or a list of them for arrays of arrays, makes a region the union of its copies. Source expressions read the copy's index as `copy` (each copy its own term and, with `total = true`, its own total), and region probes with `copies = "each"` write one reductions file per copy. Disjoint copies measure their sum in closed form (#501).
- Regions move: centers, sizes, transforms and implicit `f` may be expressions of `t`. Sources, sponges and filled-region probes follow them, with the cell fractions recomputed on the device at each stage (curved cells on their curved shapes) and totals of moving regions renormalized by device exact sums (NCCL across ranks inside CUDA graphs), so captured steps follow them without host work. Region probes write the region's measure in the domain at each write, and with `average = true` the time averages over the cells it sweeps (#501).
- Regions that cross a translational or rotational periodic boundary wrap into the domain instead of being cut off, and keep their own measures (#501).
- Conditions of regions defined by the solution, and isosurfaces' `field`, read `MACH`, `H`, `X_<species>`, `VORTICITY` (3D: `VORTICITY_X`, `_Y`, `_Z`) and `Q_CRITERION`, each cell's velocity gradient fit by least squares over the cells sharing a node with it, on the device and inside the captured step. `mallard check` places these regions on the initial solution: their measure, cells and case-view blocks at the initial time (#800).
- Filled region probes record the region's own volume and surface area (2D: area and perimeter; axisymmetric: per radian), in closed form for a primitive and otherwise to a stated tolerance, and those of its part in the domain, whose surface is the region's own inside the domain plus the domain's boundary inside it. They are in the probe sampling block, which also heads the reductions file of a probe without a samples file, in a `probe` record of `mallard.jsonl`, and in `mallard check`. A region more than 0.1% outside the domain gets a warning (`W-PROBE-OUTSIDE`) and records the share outside as `clipped` (#724).
- Snapshots of regions defined by the solution and of moving filled regions: `[[write_data]]` `region` (or `regions`) takes a `where` region, an isosurface or a moving filled region, written at each write as the cells it then covers (with `REGION_FRACTION`) or the elements then extracted. VTKHDF series (`series = true`, and the PolyData file of an isosurface) store each snapshot's mesh after the previous ones'. `mallard post extract --snapshot --region` crops to such a region on the state of saved output (#797).
- Regions defined by the solution: `{type = "where", condition = "T > 1500"}` (comparisons of `x`, `y`, `z`, `t`, `RHO`, `U_*`, `P`, `T`, `Y_<species>` joined by `and`, `or`, `not`) and `{type = "isosurface", field = "T", value = 1500}` (normals toward increasing field, `flip = true` reverses them), with booleans and `boundary` like other regions. Cell fractions are the exact clip of each cell's linear level (value at the centroid, least-squares gradient over the cells sharing a node), so they converge at second order. Sources and sponges follow them at every stage on the device, inside the captured step; probes at each write (area, flux and integrals through isosurfaces), with their cost in the run-health timing row `regions`; `mallard post extract` places them on the state of saved output. Example `hot_gas_regions` (#543).
- `mallard input resolve input.toml > full.toml` writes the input as the run uses it: every key the setup reads, with defaults and preset keys written out, quantities in plain units and keys sorted. It runs bitwise the same and resolves to itself (#509).
- The run block of every output file holds the resolved input (`input.resolved`); `tools/mallard/vtu.py` `resolved_input(path)` reads it (#509).
- `mallard -i input.toml --restart latest` (and `mallard check --restart latest`) resumes from the newest complete restart file of the input's restart writers (of the run that started last, then of the highest step), skipping with a warning a file that does not read completely, or starts from `[initialize]` when there is none. The job scripts in `tools/jobs/` and the Kubernetes manifests run it instead of looking for the newest file themselves (#510).
- A restart on a mesh file of other contents than the one that wrote the file (by the hashes in their run blocks) warns; the log's initial state says when the input differs.
- The run record's `end` record says how the run ended, as `outcome` (`finished`, `stopped`, `signal`, `wall_time_limit`, `diverged`, `error`) and `cause` (which stop condition, stop request, trigger or signal; a trigger by name in `trigger`), with the last snapshot and restart file recorded complete (`step`, `t`, `path`) and the `reason` in words. It is also written when the run diverges or fails on rank 0, so a run without one was killed. `mallard info DIR` and the run summary state the outcome (#670).
- `physics.units = "SI" | "nondimensional"` (default `SI`): a nondimensional case says so, and its output labels no number with an SI unit (the progress table's units row shows `-`). Every nondimensional example and tutorial sets it; a gas mixture is always SI (#659).
- `mallard -i input.toml --verbose`: a line for every file written, between the progress rows (#675).
- Every run writes `report.html` (`[output] report`, default on): one self-contained page of the run, built from its run record: the outcome, run identity, configuration, health (margins, activity, repairs, conservation, warnings), plots of the monitors, integrals and forces drawn in the browser (no network; at most 512 points per quantity, keeping each stretch's least and largest values), cost, the output index and the divergence report. Written at the first progress row, after each restart file and at the end, also of a run that stops, diverges or fails, and with `run_log = ""` too; `mallard info RUN_DIR --html` rebuilds the same page from `mallard.jsonl`; `mallard info RUN_DIR` prints its outcome, health, cost and warnings as text from the same records, with `mallard check`'s statuses and units (#515).
- The run record's `start` record gives the units of the progress records' quantities (`units`): of the monitors, the health margins, the `[integrals]` and each `[[forces]]` zone, as the CSV files' units lines; the run report labels every plot's axes with them.
- Runs record the check at setup in the `start` record's `check`: mesh quality and resolution as `mallard check` measures them, the CFL number against the time integrator's stability limit, the memory after setup, and the check's warnings. `report.html` and `mallard info RUN_DIR` show it with the check's statuses and units, with the largest CFL number of the progress rows against the same limit. The check's JSON gives each mesh-quality metric its `name` and `units` (#715).
- `mallard completion bash|zsh|fish` prints a tab-completion script for the shell: commands, options, `latest` and restart files for `--restart`, input files, template names for `mallard new`, run directories and requests for `mallard ctl`. It is made from the command table that makes the help pages, so new commands complete as they are added. `cmake --install` (so Spack) installs the scripts under `share/bash-completion`, `share/zsh/site-functions` and `share/fish/vendor_completions.d`, and the containers load the bash one (#507).
- Every snapshot records which snapshot it is: step, time (s), the run that wrote it (`run_id`, a UUID per run, and the run and step it restarted from), when and why it was written (`interval`, `start`, `end`, `stop`, `checkpoint`, `divergence`), its format version, the solver's precision, each field's units, description and stored precision, and for averaged fields the averaging window and sample count. It is a snapshot block in every format's own slot (VTU, PVTU and probe VTP comments, VTKHDF and XDMF HDF5 datasets, a restart trailer, the spectra CSV), next to field data `TIME` and `STEP` and the step in `.pvd`, `.vtkhdf.series` and XDMF indexes (#667).
- `mallard.jsonl` holds the run's output index: an `output` record (file, kind, step, time, reason, writer, run) for every file written, and the start record's `run_id` and `restart_of` (run log version 2). `--restart latest` finds the restart files through it (#667).
- Time-series CSV files (forces, integrals, probes, region reductions, spectra and the reactor's) mark where each run starts appending with its run block, and probe HDF5 files list each run with its first row (`mallard_runs`), so the reader gives every row its run. The reactor's CSV gains a `step` column and a units line (#667).
- `mallard info` prints the run block under **Run** (the same for every file of the run) and the last snapshot's block under **This snapshot**, with units, and each field's stored precision; `--format json` gives every snapshot's block and run (#667).
- Each snapshot block lists every field's stored precision (`float64`, `float32`) next to the solver's (#674).
- `[statistics.convergence]` follows monitored values (integrals, forces, probes) during the run: the end of the initial transient by MSER-5, confidence intervals on their means and variances by batch means (Student's t), integrated autocorrelation times and effective sample sizes, written to `convergence.csv` and the run record and carried by restart files. Triggers read them (`convergence_stationary`, `convergence_converged`, `convergence_<signal>_ci`, ...), so a run can start its statistics once the transient has ended and stop once the intervals reach a tolerance (#337).
- `[statistics] batch` gives every cell confidence intervals on its time averages by batch means, `CI_MEAN_<A>` and `CI_COV_<A>_<B>` (Student's t over the closed batches, `confidence` default 0.95), carried by restart files; off by default, it costs four times the averages' device memory (#337).
- Synthetic turbulence in initial states: `[initialize.turbulence]`, and `turbulence` in `[[initialize.regions]]`, add a divergence-free random velocity of a prescribed energy spectrum (von Kármán-Pao with an optional Kolmogorov scale, Passot-Pouquet, or a tabulated E(k) from a CSV file), given an intensity u' and an integral length scale, to any initial state, a restart or remap included, or inside a region, fading out over its edge. The field is a sum of Fourier modes on the wavevectors of a periodic box (default the mesh's bounding box), each shell holding its share of the spectrum's energy, with the modes drawn from a hash of the `seed` and the wavevector: the same bit for bit on any rank count, in 2D and 3D. Cells take its averages like other initial states. Example `turbulent_hot_spot_2d` (#563).
- `[[triggers]]`: a condition on the run (a kexpr expression of `t`, `step`, `dt`, `wall` and the latest `[integrals]`, `[[forces]]` and `[[probes]]` values) that stops the run, writes a restart or a `[[write_data]]` snapshot, or starts the statistics; once or on every rising edge, optionally held for a time, recorded in the log and `mallard.jsonl`, carried across restarts, and decided identically on every rank.
- Every snapshot says whether each field holds cell averages (`cell_average`: `RHO`, `RHOU`, `RHOE`, `RHOY_*` and their means), values computed from the cell's averaged state (`cell_state`: `P`, `T`, `U`, ...) or the reconstruction's point values (`point_value`: probes and region snapshots with `sampling = "point"`), as `value` per field in its snapshot block, with the point values' `reconstruction` (method, order, fallbacks); VTKHDF, HDF5 and XDMF datasets carry the CF attribute `cell_methods` (`area: mean`, `area: point`). `mallard info` lists them, `mallard diff` warns when A and B hold different kinds of values of a field, and `tools/mallard/vtu.py` has `value_kinds()` (#671).

### Changed

- Boundary probes on walls with a `wall_model` take `TRACTION_VISCOUS`, `TAU_W`, `CF`, `U_TAU`, `Y_PLUS` and `Q_W` from the model, as `[[forces]]` does, instead of the resolved wall gradients (#591).
- `mallard check` reports in sections whose headings end with their status, and in tables whose rows each start with one (`ok`, `WARN`, `ERROR`). Every number names its units (in a table with a `units` column, there and only there), or says it is dimensionless or in wall units, and says what it is of (which rank, what state, against which limit). The result is on the first line and on the last. Boundaries are listed by zone with condition and faces, and the worst mesh cells by metric with their global ids. Errors and warnings are collected at the end, each with a stable code (`E-REGION-OUTSIDE`, `W-CFL-LIMIT`, ...) and the docs anchor that explains it. The report goes to stdout, warnings and errors included (#657).
- GCC builds without a device backend no longer fuse multiplies and adds across statements (`-ffp-contract=off`), so arm64 gives the same results as x86-64.
- Mallard now supports the two newest major releases of GCC and Clang, the current Xcode, the two newest CUDA minor releases and the latest ROCm and oneAPI: GCC 15, Clang 22 or Apple Clang 21 (Xcode 26.4) and CMake 4.2 or newer are required (were GCC 10.4, Clang 14 and CMake 3.22), and configuring with an older compiler stops with a message that names the minimum. The Spack package refuses older compilers and needs CMake 4.2 and, with `+cuda`, CUDA 13.4 or newer. [Supported platforms](docs/installation.md#supported-platforms) lists what CI tests, and how to get a recent compiler.
- CI runs on Ubuntu 26.04 and tests GCC 15 and 16 and Clang 22 and 23 on x86-64 and arm64, Apple Clang 21 and Homebrew's GCC 16 on macOS 26, `spack install mallard %gcc@15` on Red Hat UBI 8 and 9 (as on TOSS 4 and 5 clusters), and compiles with CUDA 13.4 and 13.5 (also on arm64), ROCm 10.1 and oneAPI 2026.2.
- The container images are built on Ubuntu 26.04 (GCC 15, Open MPI 5, HDF5 1.14), the CUDA image with CUDA 13.5 (driver R580 or newer).
- A build with `Mallard_ENABLE_CGNS=ON` and Ubuntu 26.04's CGNS package (`libcgns-dev`) links: the package names HDF5's `hdf5-shared` target, which Ubuntu's HDF5 package does not define.
- The Q-criterion output variable `Q` is renamed `Q_CRITERION`, with no alias: `variables = ["Q"]` is now an input error that names `Q_CRITERION`. The examples, the `channel_les` tutorial and the example scripts write and read `Q_CRITERION` (part of #511).
- Bound-preserving TENO (`bound_preserving = true`) no longer makes premixed flames 4-6% too fast: it clipped troubled cells at smooth extrema of the nearly uniform pressure, whose overshoot is far below the pressure but not below its range among the neighbors, and fell to first order there. The neighbors' range of density and pressure is now widened by 0.1% of the cell's value; the stoichiometric H2/air flame at 700 K runs within 1% of Cantera with TENO3 and TENO5 at 3 and 6 cells per thermal thickness, as without it (#707).
- Data only the setup reads is freed before the first step: on GPUs the node coordinates and the cells' and faces' node lists of the mesh (kept for 2D TENO, which reads them), the zones' face lists and the host copy of the vertex-neighbor lists; the regions' volume fractions; and the host memory the setup freed, which is handed back to the system (Linux). On one A100 a 2D MUSCL run holds 5% less device memory, and 3D TENO runs hold 0.85-1.65 GB less host memory per million cells; `mallard check`'s memory after setup counts what remains. Results are unchanged.
- **Strang-split chemistry fuses each step's closing half step with the next step's opening one by default** (`[chemistry] fuse_half_steps = true`): one chemistry call per step. Output, checks, probes, statistics and triggers read the state with the pending half step applied, on a copy the run does not continue from, and restart files hold the state before it with its time (attribute `CHEMISTRY_PENDING`), so results no longer depend on the output schedule, and a run restarted at any step, or stopped by `t_wall_stop`, reproduces the uninterrupted one bitwise. Results change within the chemistry's tolerances; `fuse_half_steps = false` keeps the separate half steps, and `mallard input upgrade` notes inputs on the new default. On GPUs the steps with progress rows, monitors, means or probes of fused runs are taken one step at a time (#777).
- `[run] control_file_interval`: a `STOP` or `CHECKPOINT` file acts at the first step whose time-step reduction reads the request, up to `[run] step_queue` steps after the step at which the host found it, as `mallard ctl` requests and signals do (#730).
- GPU performance: boundary conditions dispatch on a case per condition chosen at setup (teno5_3d +0.9% on A100); the mixture scalar kernels leave the rotational-periodicity code out on meshes without rotational pairs, and the chemistry clipping audit adds no live state to the reactor integration (flame_3d +3.3% together) (#613).
- Characteristic (`nscbc`) boundaries take their steps in the CUDA graph (CUDA builds): the incoming waves advance over the step's dt on the device, and inlet values of `t` are evaluated at the stage and step-end times on the device.
- Reacting gas mixtures (Strang and SIMPLER splitting, fused half steps, TFLES, PaSR, chemistry load balancing) take their steps in the CUDA graph (CUDA builds): the chemistry's time step, queue, its order by cost, failure count and load-balancing plan stay on the device. A CUDA graph now covers every step whose halos go through NCCL.
- `[chemistry] load_balance`: each pair of ranks moves at most a capacity of cells per call. The capacity starts at 0 and follows the cells the pair asked for, so the first step and a balanced run move no cells.
- Synthetic inflow turbulence takes its steps in the CUDA graph (CUDA builds): the inflow planes are picked, generated and filtered on the device from the step's time on the device.
- In GPU builds the interpolation weights of the inflow planes in time are computed on the device: results of synthetic-turbulence runs on GPUs can differ from earlier versions in the last digits.
- LES runs take their steps in the CUDA graph (CUDA builds): the dynamic constant of `dynamic = true` and the dynamic thickened-flame efficiency are computed on the device, and calc_dt's boundary states use the step's time on the device.
- In GPU builds the dynamic thickened-flame exponent `beta` is computed with the device's `log`: results of `efficiency = "dynamic"` runs on GPUs can differ from earlier versions in the last digits.
- Halo exchanges of states of different widths share one set of buffers, sized for the widest, instead of reallocating them at each change of width.
- Gas mixtures without chemistry (also with double flux and LES) take their steps in the CUDA graph (CUDA builds): their time step is reduced on the device, and double flux freezes and resets the cells' energy inside the graph.
- The key catalog is `docs/input-keys/`, one file per key (its path, percent-encoded outside lowercase letters, digits, `_`, `.` and `-`, plus `.json`), so pull requests that add different keys change different files; `tools/dev/input_catalog.py build` writes it, and `mallard list keys` and the install read it (#845).
- Faster analytical and layered initialization (`[initialize]` expressions and `[[initialize.regions]]`). On GPUs the cells' simplices are built in the kernel from the mesh, not on the host and copied over, and launches take as many cells as 64 MB of scratch memory holds: a Taylor-Green start on 1M hexahedra takes 0.3 s instead of 3.2 s on an A100. CPU builds evaluate the base state's expressions 14 points at a time (7 in 2D): 2.8x faster than before on the same case (M4 Pro, 2 threads), on par with 0.8's exprtk. Results are bitwise unchanged.
- Every expression is evaluated by Mallard's compiled expression engine, with the same code on CPUs and GPUs: sponges (strength, state and composition), boundary `where` filters, mass-flow `profile`s and boundary compositions now evaluate on the device at setup. Their values can change in the last bits: exprtk evaluated them with strength reduction, which rewrites some chains of divisions (`(a / b) / c` as `a / (b * c)`, ...), and where the compiler fuses multiply-adds (Apple silicon) its multiply-add nodes rounded once; both now round as written, the same on every CPU. Probe curves and surfaces and synthetic-inflow profiles keep their values ([Expressions](docs/guide/input_files.md#expressions)).
- The target `p` of an average-pressure outlet (`average = true`), when it depends on `t`, is evaluated on the device with the other boundary values of `t`: the outlet no longer copies its condition to the host and back at every stage. Results are bitwise unchanged.
- Expressions that use exprtk's vector, string or bitwise functions (`dot`, `like`, `shl`, ...) or its case-insensitive names (`X` for `x`) fail with an error that names the problem and, for names, the one to use ([Expressions](docs/guide/expressions.md)).
- The expression language is named kexpr (Kokkos expressions) and has its own guide page, [Expressions](docs/guide/expressions.md).
- TENO with `convective_flux = "hybrid"` feeds the central flux the cells' values (`central = "cell_averages"`) instead of the reconstructed states, which added kinetic energy, for a single gas and for gas mixtures: on Comte-Bellot & Corrsin at 128³ with Sigma the spectral error is 0.076 / 0.056 at stations 2 / 3, against 0.19 / 0.22 for TENO5 + HLLC. Set `central = "reconstructed"` for the earlier flux; MUSCL and first order are unchanged (#689, #801).
- In NCCL builds, steps that run without the CUDA graph communicate between ranks with NCCL on the GPU's stream, as graphed steps do: the time step and run-control requests, the step error of `[numerics.error_control]`, the dynamic LES constant and thickened-flame efficiency, chemistry load balancing (loads and cells), the chemistry failure count, `check_nan` and the signal of triggered phase averages. `halo_exchange = "mpi"` and builds without NCCL use MPI for the same operations; results are the same bit for bit.
- Distributed reacting runs no longer reduce chemistry timings over the ranks at every chemistry call: the summary's "chemistry of the slowest rank" is now the largest of the ranks' total chemistry times (before: the sum over calls of each call's slowest rank), and the health report's chemistry `imbalance` is the same ratio over the interval.
- Average-pressure outlets and sources of domain averages no longer keep steps out of CUDA graphs: their sums over the ranks run on the device, with NCCL on the GPU's stream in NCCL builds.
- The area-averaged pressure of `average = true` outlets is an exact (fixed-point) sum: results differ from earlier versions in the last digits.
- Spectra statistics (`[statistics.spectra]`) of distributed runs move their slabs between the ranks and gather the plane sums with NCCL on the GPU's stream in NCCL builds (MPI with `halo_exchange = "mpi"` and in builds without NCCL), with the same results.
- **Changed behavior:** an input that sets neither `[numerics] preset` nor `[numerics.face_reconstruction]` now runs the `high_order` preset (TENO-E 5, HLLC, SSPRK3, and `cfl = 1.0` when `[run]` sets neither `cfl` nor `dt`) instead of first order. Keys the input sets still win, and the terminal output and the run block's `preset.default` say the default was used. To reproduce older first-order results, add `[numerics.face_reconstruction] type = "FO"`; `mallard input upgrade` prints a note for the inputs this concerns and leaves them unchanged. The presets are named by their order (`second_order`, `high_order`; see Deprecated), and the new `first_order` preset (FO, HLL, SSPRK3, `cfl = 0.5`) is for debugging, start-up transients and the last fallback. The numerics guide shows, measured, why high order is the default: on smooth flow it reaches a given error at a fraction of the cost.
- Faster setup, with unchanged results: cut planes, lines and other flat probes skip the cells a plane does not cross before cutting them (a cut plane of a 100^3 mesh sets up in 0.17 s instead of 1.34 s); building a 3D mesh takes half as long (1.2 s instead of 2.4 s for 100^3 hexahedra: cell orientation, cell geometry, vertex neighbors and the face quadrature without per-cell or per-face allocations); and the boundary setup lists the cells of each node only when a boundary needs image points (0.02 s instead of 0.15 s on a periodic box).
- **The `les` numerics preset runs `SSPRK54` at `cfl = 2.0`** instead of `SSPRK3` at 0.8: the same spectra on `cbc_les` and the same Taylor-Green decay for two thirds of the right-hand-side evaluations. To reproduce earlier results, set `[numerics] time_integrator = "SSPRK3"` and, if the input sets no `cfl`, `[run] cfl = 0.8`; `mallard input upgrade` notes the change for inputs with `preset = "les"` and no `time_integrator`. The `high_order` preset keeps `SSPRK3`; the numerics guide gives the comparison (#774).
- **Probes no longer read samples outside the domain from the nearest cell.** A `point` or `points` probe with a point outside the domain is now an input error that names the point and its distance to the nearest boundary zone: move the point inside. Curves and resampled surfaces drop their samples outside the domain with a warning (`W-PROBE-OUTSIDE`, also in `mallard.jsonl`) and record the dropped samples as `clipped` in the probe file, and one entirely outside is an error. A point on a boundary face counts as inside (#673).
- Warnings issued during setup (deprecated keys, clipped probes, output formats) are also written to `mallard.jsonl`, right after the run's `start` record (#673).
- Gas mixtures: `[statistics]` sampling and point probes solve each cell's temperature once per sample, where they solved it twice (#513).
- `[[write_data]] format = "xdmf"` writes XDMF indexes over HDF5 files (the mesh once, a file of fields per snapshot), for VisIt, meshio and ParaView of any version: the files `format = "hdf5"` wrote, now a supported format rather than a deprecated one. `docs/guide/output.md` has a table of which format to choose.
- The run block's `input.resolved` replaces the provenance's `setup` block (the terminal setup summary), and the preset values are the resolved input's TOML (`"\"HLLC\""`). The key catalog, the run block's preset and the log's preset line are made from the same key records as the resolved input (#509).
- Input tables are iterated in key order, not hash order: results no longer depend on the order of the keys in the file (e.g. the species of a composition `X = {...}`) or on the standard library. Runs whose input iterates a table, such as mixtures with a composition, change in the last bits (#509).
- The run's setup banner is in tables: each section's settings, then its quantities with their units in a column (cells, faces, the time step or CFL number, the stops, output and probe intervals, the hybrid flux's threshold, chemistry tolerances), the mesh's extent, cell sizes and owned cells per rank as min and max, the partition's ranks, halo layers and exchange sizes as rows, the boundaries as zone, condition, faces and details, and the outputs as file, interval with its units and contents. The models' constants are quantities with their units too: the gas's gamma, R, cp and cv, the viscosity law's, the source terms' (gravity, mass flow, region areas or volumes, sponges, radiation), TENO's stencil sizes and switch, the MUSCL limiter's, the step controller's tolerances and PID coefficients, and LES's, TFLES's and PaSR's. `TENO setup` does not repeat the Setup section's numerics time, and durations below a millisecond use the micro sign in a UTF-8 locale (#659, #675, #826, #849).
- The progress table of a run gives the units of its columns in a row under their names, keeps one unit per column (`wall/step` in ms, `throughput` in cells/s, `ETA` as h:mm:ss) so every column reads straight down, and opens with a note on what the columns are. Files written no longer interrupt it: its last column, `written`, counts them since the previous row (`2 vtu, 1 restart`); `mallard.jsonl` and the summary name them. Requests acted on and a divergence print a remark between the rows (`>> step 339, t = 0.061 s: stop: ...`) (#659, #675).
- The end-of-run Summary and Health sections are tables: wall time by phase with its share, throughput, bytes written and files by kind; the lowest density, pressure and temperature with their units, step and location; activity by rank, repairs and the conservation ledger (#659).
- Run health's `limited_cells` counts a MUSCL cell only when its smallest limiter is below 0.9, not below 1: smooth limiters such as Venkatakrishnan's sit just below 1 almost everywhere in smooth flow, so the old count was near 100% for any smooth run.
- The provenance block is now the run block: schema `mallard-run` version 3, the XML comment `<!-- mallard-run {...} -->`, the HDF5 attribute `mallard_run` and the CSV line `# mallard-run {...}`, with `run.run_id` and `run.restart_of`. Files of earlier releases, with the old names, are read as before, with a note (#667).
- Restart files are written as version 7 (a snapshot block trailer before the run block's), which Mallard 0.9 and earlier reject with their newer-version message; every earlier version is still read (#667).
- Step, time and run come from what files record, never from file names: XDMF members from their data items, the mesh file of XDMF output from its attribute, `--restart latest` from the output index (a listing of `<prefix>_*.restart` ordered by what the files record for run logs before 0.10, said in its message), and `tools/mallard`'s `series()` in the order of the recorded time (#667).
- Spectra files record their step, time and averaging window in a snapshot block line in place of the free-text `# step = ...` line, and the divergence report names the run id (#667).
- File writes are `output` records of `mallard.jsonl` instead of `event` records (#667).
- **Runs with time-dependent boundaries, inflows, sources or sponges under `SSPRK54`, `RDPK3Sp35` or `CK45` change in the last bits**: each stage time t + c dt is rounded once, the same on CPUs and GPUs and whether or not steps are queued ahead. The earlier rounding cannot be restored (#944).
- The other commands' errors have stable codes from the catalog runs and `mallard check` use, each with a line naming the docs that explain it: usage errors `E-USAGE`, `E-DIMENSION`, `E-EXECUTABLE` and `E-ONE-RANK` from the front end, and `E-INFO`, `E-LIST`, `E-DIFF`, `E-MESH-CONVERT`, `E-INPUT-UPGRADE`, `E-EXTRACT`, `E-CONVERGENCE`, `E-PACK` from their commands; deprecated options warn with `W-OPTION-DEPRECATED` (#828).
- Every warning and error of a run has a stable code (`W-HEALTH-MARGIN`, `E-DIVERGED`, `E-INPUT`, ...) from the catalog `mallard check` uses, and a line with the docs that explain it; the run record's `warning` and `error` records give `code` and `docs`, and `report.html` lists warnings with their codes (#659).
- The chemistry benchmark's CSV (`mallard reactor` with `[benchmark]`) has a units line under its header, as every other CSV output (#659).
- **The 2D TENO setup runs on the device**, as the 3D one does: GPU runs set up their stencil tables there, one thread per cell in batches of at most 16,384 cells, so the setup's workspace stays within the run's own peak, and CPU runs share the code. The device and the host give bitwise the same tables. On CPUs the setup is faster: 1.5 times from the shared code (524,288 triangles at order 5, 2 threads: 44.9 s to 29.4 s), then 1.7 times in 2D and 1.1 times in 3D at order 5 from fewer operations per table entry (#916). **The tables change at round-off**: the order 5 and 6 smoothness-indicator matrices take their powers by multiplication, and arm64 Clang builds compute every 2D table without fused multiply-adds. TENO caches become `MALLARD-TENO-14`; older ones are recomputed (#776, #938).
- TENO sets up on meshes one cell thick (2D strips, 3D slabs and pencils, such as quasi-1D shock tubes, flames and detonations), where orders above 3 (and order 3 on 3D pencils) stopped with "could not build a full-rank large stencil": fits there drop the monomials of the axes the mesh has no neighbors across and keep the design order along the others. TENO3 on such meshes now fits the same way, which changes its results there slightly.
- Probe reductions are faster: each reduction loops over the samples once, without branches, and the gathered samples reuse their host buffers from write to write. On one A100 the probes_3d benchmark spends 61 ms per step on output, down from 70 ms. Results are unchanged.
- **`[[write_data]]` VTKHDF output defaults to `series = true`**: every snapshot goes into one file, `PREFIX.vtkhdf`, which ParaView opens as the time series, instead of `PREFIX_<n>.vtkhdf` files and their `PREFIX.vtkhdf.series` index. Set `series = false` for one file per snapshot; `mallard input upgrade` notes each VTKHDF `[[write_data]]` without `series`. The Python package reads the snapshots of a series file: `mallard.vtu.read_index("solut/flow")` lists them, each a path that `read_vtu_cells` and the other readers read as that snapshot, `mallard.vtu.snapshot("solut/flow")` gives the last, and `mallard.vtu.read_pyvista` reads one with PyVista (#768).
- Distributed TENO runs reconstruct only the cells each rank owns: the far side of each partition face comes from the rank that owns it, sent while the other faces' fluxes are computed, and the first stage's interior reconstruction overlaps the time-step exchange and reduction. 3D TENO5 weak scaling on 16 A100s goes from 91% to 96.5%, with bitwise the same results. Meshes with rotational periodicity (#911), and LES with `gradients = "reconstruction"` (#912), overlap less (#305).

### Deprecated

- `mallard new --list`: use `mallard list templates`. It still lists them, with a notice.
- `[[write_data]] variables` and `[[probes]] variables` are renamed `fields`, the name `[statistics]` already uses: the old name is read with a warning until 0.11.0, and `mallard input upgrade` rewrites it. The examples, tutorials, templates and docs use `fields`; `[[probes]] spectra` (new in this release) takes `fields` too (#502).
- `mallard check --quality PREFIX` is renamed `--mesh-fields PREFIX`; `--quality` still works, with a warning (#521).
- `[numerics] preset = "robust"` and `"accurate"` are renamed `"second_order"` and `"high_order"`: the old names are read with a warning until 0.11.0, and `mallard input upgrade` renames them.

### Removed

- exprtk is no longer bundled or built: Mallard's expression language, which follows its syntax and numerics, has its own literal conversion and derivative. The input syntax is unchanged; exprtk's MIT notice stays in `licenses/exprtk-MIT.txt` and NOTICE.
- `tools/post/plane_average.py`: `mallard post average --statistics --homogeneous x,z` replaces it, on any mesh, with the same CSV columns (#512).
- The input keys and options deprecated in 0.9.0 are removed: a run (and `mallard check`) with one stops with an error naming its replacement, and `mallard input upgrade old.toml > new.toml` still rewrites inputs of 0.8 and earlier. Removed: the `[[boundaries]]` types `upt`, `p_out`, `p_out_average`, `extrapolation`, `nscbc_inlet` and `nscbc_outlet` (use `inlet_state`, `outlet_pressure`, `outlet_pressure` with `average = true`, `outlet_extrapolation`, and `inlet_state` / `outlet_pressure` with an `nscbc` table), and the characteristic parameters `L`, `sigma`, `beta`, `sigma_T`, `sigma_t` outside the `nscbc` table; `[[mesh.curved]]` `shape` (use `type`, with `filled = false` for a sphere or cylinder); `[[write_data]]` `geometry` (use `region`, `"all"` being `"domain"`); `[mesh] periodic` and `[initialize.mesh] periodic` (use `[[periodic]]` and `[[initialize.periodic]]` zone pairs).
- `[[write_data]] format = "hdf5"`, ambiguous since VTKHDF is HDF5 too: use `"vtkhdf"` (the default, ParaView 5.12+) or `"xdmf"`, its new name, for the same XDMF and HDF5 files. `mallard input upgrade` rewrites it to `"vtkhdf"`. `[[probes]] format = "hdf5"` is unchanged.

### Fixed

- The case view's `.vtm` (builds without HDF5) escapes the quotes in block names such as `probes[name = "p"].region`, which made it unreadable (#546).
- The initial solution's setup peak no longer holds two chunks' cell geometry at once on meshes initialized in several chunks.
- Runs whose ranks exchange, gather or write more than 2^31 values at once no longer fail or corrupt data: distributed setup, halo exchanges, chemistry load balancing, string broadcasts and gathers, MPI-IO restart writes and the rank placement report send long messages in pieces, and collectives take 64-bit totals (#775, #876, #894).
- `mallard input upgrade` no longer crashes when built with GCC on macOS (#692).
- The error of a boundary value of `t` out of range (a `p`, `T`, `mass_flux`, composition or direction of `t`, a mass-flow `profile` or `mdot`) names the same face on every run: of the faces that failed together, the one with the lowest index, rather than whichever thread got there first (#919).
- When several input expressions stop at the iteration limit together, the error names the same one on every run, rather than one that depended on thread scheduling (#928).
- Run-control commands submitted on a rank other than 0 no longer stay pending after the run, where the next solver of the process acted on them in collectives the other ranks never reached and hung (#946).
- Snapshot metadata: covariances of a y component with another field (`COV_U_Y_U_Y`, `COV_RHOU_Y_P`) carry the units and long name of their two fields, not of a vector and a mass fraction, and a region's `REGION_FRACTION` is listed once (#795).
- GCC 15 builds with `-DMallard_WARNINGS_AS_ERRORS=ON` no longer fail on a possibly dangling reference in the mechanism reader.
- Messages that print a count followed by a noun use the singular for a count of one ("1 cell", "1 step", "1 rank"), in the solver log, `mallard check`, the divergence report and the `mallard` subcommands (#869).
- On meshes with curved boundaries, regions follow the curved walls: volume fractions are of the curved cells, points (probe samples, exact surfaces' Gauss points) are located in the curved cells, boundary zones and the faces around regions of the mesh clipped by a region are cut on the curved faces, and extracted surfaces and planes are cut from the curved cells. Before, the slivers between the straight faces and a wall were left out or counted in the wrong place, and points beyond a convex wall were sampled (#724).
- Clipped surfaces and curves end where the exact surface crosses the mesh's boundary, not at the last Gauss point inside it, and mesh faces and extracted elements are clipped at the roots of the clipping region's distance instead of linearly between their vertices (#724).
- The repairs' changes in progress rows and the run record, and the conservation ledger's `repairs`, `residual` and `relative` terms and the element drift derived from them, are the same bit for bit between identical runs and at any `step_queue` depth: the chemistry's composition clippings are summed by a reduction instead of atomics (#782).
- 3D TENO runs whose troubled cells took more than one round (`troubled_capacity` below 1, the 3D default 0.5) could differ in the last bits from run to run, or between rank counts. Which cells the first round took followed the order of the queue's atomic increments, and those cells kept the smooth pass's central coefficients while the rest recomputed them in another kernel, whose FMAs could round differently (seen on GPUs with rotational periodicity). Every round now recomputes them.
- `t_wall_stop` in parallel runs: every rank stops after the same step, the next one after any rank reaches the limit, where ranks whose clocks differed could stop at different steps and hang (#670).
- A run whose `t_stop` is a multiple of a `time_interval` that rounds just below it (for example 11 × 0.03 for `t_stop = 0.33`) took one more step of a round-off's length and wrote a second final snapshot. `t_stop` now counts as reached within round-off, so the run ends on the snapshot at `t_stop`.
- The Python tools read the restart files Mallard writes: `tile_restart.py`, `interpolate_restart.py`, `channel_init.py`, `isotropic_turbulence_restart.py`, `insitu_statistics.py`, `tools/docs/tools.py` and the example scripts rejected some versions 4 to 6. They share one reader and writer, `tools/mallard/restart.py`, which reads every version from 2 on, compressed files too, and CI checks it against the restart files of the current build.
- The output guide says how to open XDMF output in ParaView 6.0.1, whose reader fails on a bare `.xmf` file name (`flow.xmf`) but reads `./flow.xmf` or any path with a directory (#682).

- Sponge regions: `[[sponges]]` `region = {...}` scales the `strength` by the region's cell fractions, smooth `edge` included (#499).

In 3D, the surface of a region (`region = {type = "sphere", ..., filled = false}`, any surface of one primitive, or a flat surface such as a `disk` with `inner_radius`) is resampled on an `n_points = [n_u, n_v]` grid along its parameters, and a parametric `type = "surface"` likewise. Region probes over surfaces and curves without `n_points` sample at their elements' quadrature points. Surfaces, cut planes and 2D curves have the flux variables of boundary probes along their normal, so `reductions = ["integral"]` gives mass, momentum and enthalpy flow through a section (#318, #395, #499).

- `[[probes]]` sample point sets (`points`, `points_file`) and curves (`polyline`, `circle`, `arc`, parametric `curve`), with `translate`, `rotate` and `scale`; `sampling = "point"` reads TENO's reconstruction at each sample (converging at the scheme's order); `time_interval` outputs; `hdf5` and `vtp` (ParaView) formats, versioned; and `reductions` (`integral`, `mean`, `favre`, `min`, `max`, `rms`) along the geometry. Files are bitwise the same on any number of ranks (#318, #358).
- The key catalog (`mallard check --catalog`) lists the accepted values of `parallel.partitioner`, `parallel.halo_exchange`, `physics.gas` and `numerics.convective_flux`.
- Regions of the mesh: `domain` (every cell), every boundary zone (a surface; a curve in 2D) and every cell zone of a mesh file (Gmsh physical groups of cells, Fluent cell zones, CGNS families, OpenFOAM `cellZones`, kept by `mallard mesh convert`) are regions by name, without a `[regions]` entry, in sources, sponges, region probes and booleans; a `[regions]` name that clashes with one, or a cell zone named like a boundary zone or `domain`, is an error (#499).
- Boundaries and interiors: `{type = "boundary", of = ...}` is a region's boundary, one dimension lower, and `{type = "interior", of = ...}` the filled region a closed surface (2D: curve) or closed set of boundary zones encloses; `filled = false` on a filled region or flat surface is its boundary. `circle` and `ellipse` are curves, and in 3D `disk`, `annulus`, `rectangle` and `polygon` flat surfaces (`normal`, `reference`). Wrong dimensions, boundaries of closed regions, interiors of open ones and `edge` on surfaces and curves are errors (#499).
- `mallard check` lists every region a case uses and the mesh's own (domain, boundary zones, cell zones) with dimension, measure, cells and, for surfaces and curves, exact or extracted; a region covering no cell is an error naming its extent and the domain's. `mallard check --view DIR` writes the case view `DIR/case.vtkhdf` (a VTKHDF collection with one block per region and boundary zone, with fractions, normals and measures; `case.vtm` without HDF5) for ParaView (#499).
- Surfaces and curves on the mesh: region probes take surfaces, curves and points (weighted by their area, length or count in each cell). Primitives' surfaces and 3D flat surfaces and curves are exact (sampled on their parameters, exact measures where unclipped); planes, boundary zones and faces of mesh regions come exactly from the mesh; boundaries of booleans and `implicit` regions are extracted from the cells at a settable `resolution` (#499).
- Clipping across dimensions: `intersection` and `difference` of a surface or curve with filled regions keep the part inside (outside) them, e.g. the part of a wall zone in a box; clipped primitives' surfaces are integrated at high order up to the exact clipping boundary (#499).
- Orientation: closed surfaces face out, boundary zones out of the domain, flat surfaces along `normal`, 2D curves to the right of travel, 3D curves counterclockwise about their normal; `flip = true` reverses a surface or curve (#499).
- Regions: the shared syntax for parts of space (spheres, cylinders, cones, boxes, tori, half-spaces, polygons, implicit `f(x, y, z) < 0`, their unions, intersections and differences, with `scale`, `rotate`, `translate` and an optional smooth `edge`), with exact cell volume fractions, in [docs/input.md](docs/input.md#regions); the keys that take a region follow in later releases (#317, #359).
- Snapshots of regions: `[[write_data]]` `region = ...` (or `regions = [...]`, a series per region) writes a filled region as the subset of cells it covers, with only their nodes and `REGION_FRACTION`; a boundary zone, the surfaces and curves of `[regions]` (one sample at each element of the region on the mesh) and probe types given as regions (`type = "cut_plane"`, `"line"`, `"points"`, ...) as the point values a `sampling = "point"` probe writes (cell fields from each sample's cell), in VTKHDF or VTK files, the same on any number of ranks; every region is written again at the end of the run. `[[probes]]` `region` also takes probe types as regions (#542).
- Rotational periodicity: `[[periodic]]` pairs zones by a rotation (`angle`, `center`, and in 3D `axis`) as well as by a translation, with vectors and gradients turned across the pair in every scheme (MUSCL, TENO, viscous fluxes, LES, statistics), and a generated `annulus` mesh type (`r_inner`, `r_outer`, `angle`, periodic in `theta` and in 3D `z`), with the `rotational_sector` example (#315).
- Run control: `SIGUSR1` writes a restart and continues; `SIGTERM`, `SIGINT` and `SIGUSR2` write a restart after the current step and exit with status 3; `mallard ctl <run-dir> status | checkpoint | stop | set <key>=<value>` talks to a running job through a private socket; optional `STOP` and `CHECKPOINT` files (`[run] control_socket`, `control_file_interval`) (#322, #355).
- Divergence detection: every step's time-step reduction flags non-finite states, and the run stops naming the first bad cells and writing the last valid state (`[run] keep_last_good`) (#355).
- Run health diagnostics (`[output] health`, on by default) in every progress record of the run log and a Health section at the end of the human log: smallest density, pressure, temperature and mass fraction with their location and step, TENO troubled, MUSCL limited and hybrid upwind fractions per rank, chemistry sub-steps, rejections and rank imbalance, and output time per rank (#435).
- An independent conservation ledger: domain totals of mass, momentum, energy and element masses against boundary transfers re-evaluated from the boundary faces, re-evaluated sources and the recorded numerical repairs; closes at round-off for conservative setups and flags an unexplained change (#435).
- Audit of the numerical repairs (mass-fraction clipping in the chemistry, the double-flux energy reset): activations, distinct cells and the change they made (#435).
- Provenance: versions of every dependency (MPI library, HDF5, CGNS, zlib, NCCL, CUDA/HIP runtime and driver) and the host count; the run log's start record holds each rank's host, GPU and CPU set, and the setup phase times per rank (#435).
- A JSON-lines run log (`[output] run_log`, default `mallard.jsonl`): a start record with the provenance, a progress record at every progress row (step, time, CFL, throughput, ETA, monitors, memory) and an end record with the wall-time breakdown (#326, #376).
- Provenance in every output file (VTU, PVTU, HDF5, restart, CSV monitors and probes): version, git state, build configuration, resolved numerics preset, input text and hash, mesh hash, ranks and devices; `provenance(path)` of `tools/mallard/vtu.py` reads it (#326, #376).
- `[[sources]]`: any number of mass, momentum, energy and species sources, each a number, quantity or expression of position, time, the local solution (`rho`, `u_x`, `p`, `T`, `Y_<species>`, ...) and domain averages (`mean_ke`, ...), restricted to a [region](docs/input.md#regions) and optionally given as an exact integrated total (`total = true`); evaluated on the device every stage without leaving CUDA graphs. `[[write_data]]` writes them as `SOURCE_*` (#317, #384).

### Fixed

- With `double_flux` and `navier_stokes`, the diffusive energy flux reached only one side of each face (steady conduction between walls ended 500 K off) (#351). Changes results of double-flux viscous runs only.
- Distributed runs with `double_flux` and chemistry differed from serial ones by about 1e-7: the halo is refreshed before freezing the thermodynamics (#378).
- The 3D TENO setup no longer fails with "does not fit in memory" on faceted curved boundaries that touch more than 32 mirror planes, such as the far field of `sphere_mach3` (#392).
- A fast inflow into gas at rest no longer blows up the first step (#374).
- `cmake --install --prefix` is honoured (install destinations were absolute), and GoogleTest is no longer installed (#387).
- Mallard compiles with Kokkos' HIP and SYCL backends: the `_r` literal is `consteval` (no `long double` reaches a SYCL device), and the TENO kernels that only GPU builds compile convert their team indices explicitly and capture what their compile-time lambdas use (#402).
- Intel's oneAPI compiler gets Mallard's warnings, the TENO setup's `-ffp-contract=off` and the precise floating-point model, so NaN checks work (#402).
- Single-precision builds compile with Clang again: the wall-model input no longer promotes `float` to `double` implicitly, and CI's Clang build is now single precision so this stays covered (#491).
- `mallard test --help` no longer hangs where MPI cannot start without its launcher (as in the test suite under HPC-X's `mpirun`): it no longer runs a test suite for GoogleTest's page, and `mallard-test2d`/`mallard-test3d --help` start neither MPI nor the device.
- `[mesh] curved_geometry = false` leaves `[[mesh.curved]]` out instead of stopping the run on its keys as unused, as documented (#407).
- Single-precision builds read the center, radius and axis of closed-form `[[mesh.curved]]` circles, spheres and cylinders in double, as the curved geometry is computed, instead of rounding them to single precision first (projections were off by about 1e-8).
- CGNS built from source (`-DUSE_SYSTEM_CGNS=OFF`) compiles against a parallel HDF5 (#328).
- `[source] mass_flow` on CUDA builds: the force's domain sums and the step's `dt` are now taken on the device, so runs whose steps are captured as a CUDA graph no longer replay a force frozen at capture, and a restarted run reproduces the uninterrupted one bitwise. The sums are exact (fixed point), so the forced flow is also bitwise the same on any number of ranks.
- A run with an HDF5 `[[probes]]` file and HDF5 `[[write_data]]` output (written in the background by default) could crash: the probe and the background writer called the HDF5 library at the same time. Every HDF5 call now takes one shared lock.
- On several ranks, a probe file that rank 0 cannot create or continue (for example one from a newer Mallard on a restart) now stops every rank with the error, instead of leaving the others waiting. `ProbesTest` runs on 1 to 4 ranks in CI.
- Extrapolation boundaries are second order on tetrahedra and pyramids: gradient stencils continue across the boundary through the translated image cells, as across a periodic seam, instead of a mirrored ghost of the cell itself, and pyramids find their image faces one cell deep rather than at their apex. A channel with extrapolation sides converged at first order and carried a spurious cross flow (#420).
- MPI runs with a modeled wall and without `[les]` now compute the viscous gradients of the halo cells holding exchange and edge points, which the wall model read stale, so they match serial runs again (#422).
- A malformed input with an unclosed nested array before a key (as in `s=["",["",""[` then a line `[=`) no longer hangs the input reader; it reports the TOML syntax error.
- Malformed input with deeply nested or unclosed arrays and inline tables, a comment inside one (`s=["",[""[#`), or a key on the line of a nested one (`s=[` then a line `"",[""[=`) no longer crashes or hangs the input reader; it reports the TOML syntax error. Tables, arrays and inline tables nested more than 64 deep are an input error naming the key and line, instead of a stack overflow.
- Malformed mesh files are rejected with an error instead of crashing, hanging or exhausting memory: Gmsh files that end early or announce counts past their end, Fluent ids from 0, OpenFOAM files with NUL bytes, oversized lists or faces of missing cells, Mallard HDF5 meshes with bad offsets or node ids, and CGNS sections with bad element ranges or offsets (#412).
- Sources that read domain averages (`mean_rho`, `mean_ke`, ...) no longer depend on the number of ranks or threads: the averages are exact sums, as the dynamic LES and flame-thickening constants already were.
- On several ranks, an input error found in one part of the mesh (a negative mass flux, an inlet `direction` pointing out of the domain, a boundary face assigned twice, a synthetic-turbulence inlet that is not planar or a Reynolds stress that is not positive semidefinite) is reported by every rank, instead of ending the run in an MPI error or a hang.
- The error for a `[[boundaries]]` or `[[forces]]` zone missing from the mesh lists every zone of the mesh on any number of ranks, not the zones of one rank's part (with the internal `__partition__`).
- A boundary value given as an expression that leaves its range (a temperature or pressure that is not positive, a negative mass flux, a zero composition), at setup or during the run, stops every rank with its error, not just the ranks holding the offending faces.
- Probe VTP files hold their field data (`MALLARD_FORMAT_VERSION`, the units record) at the data-set level, where VTK and ParaView read it, instead of inside the piece (#518).
- Bound-preserving TENO (`bound_preserving = true`) keeps density and pressure positive in every cell at `cfl` up to 0.5 in planar single-gas runs: the convective fluxes are blended toward first order on the faces where the update would leave a cell without positive density or pressure. Its face values alone did not guarantee this, and the Mach 8 `mallard` example on its cubic mesh diverged in the near vacuum behind the tail. Faces that need no limiting are unchanged (#603).
- The `difference` region examples of the docs are valid TOML (an inline table cannot span lines); a CI check parses every TOML snippet of the docs (#499).
- Restart files are written as `<name>.restart.partial`, flushed to storage, and renamed once every rank has written its part, so a file named `.restart` is always complete: a run killed while writing one, or a script picking the newest restart while one was being written in the background, no longer finds a truncated file.
- A run resumed from a restart file whose snapshot index (`.vtkhdf.series`, `.pvd`, `.xmf`) was missing, such as one copied to another machine with only its restart file, numbered its snapshots from 0 again and overwrote the earlier ones. Restart files now count each `time_interval` writer's snapshots, so numbering continues after the restart time regardless.
- Characteristic (NSCBC) boundaries of gas mixtures read the current stage's reconstructed `gamma`, not the previous stage's (zero at the first step) (#355).
- The `start` record of a run resumed from a restart file did not say which restart file it started from, so a run record (`mallard.jsonl`) continued several times could not be told apart from a rewind. It now holds `restart` (`file`, `step`, `t`), and `rewound_from` when the run resumes from an earlier restart file than the last step recorded.
- Spack package: `+nccl` passes `cuda_arch` on to NCCL, so `mallard+cuda+nccl cuda_arch=<N>` concretizes.
- A value of the wrong type (`Nx = 16.0`, `Nx = "16"`, `keep_last_good = "false"`) or a negative count is an error instead of silently taking the default or wrapping around; an integer is accepted where a number is expected.
- `run.cfl` and `run.dt` must be positive, and the forces and integrals files must open, instead of failing later.

- `extrapolation` and characteristic boundary faces without an image face (most faces of unstructured triangles and tetrahedra) take the image cell's TENO-E polynomial at their translated quadrature points instead of its average: against TENO's high-order interior state the average grew round-off in a uniform subsonic inflow into vacuum states and NaNs.

- The 3D TENO setup runs on the GPU, 6–9.5× faster, bitwise identical (#247); 3D TENO device memory is about 15% lower (#265); MUSCL reconstruction is fused and overlapped with the halo exchange (#239); halo exchanges carry only the cells the stencils read (#258); reacting mixtures are 2× faster per step (#257, #264); large mechanisms solve faster on GPUs (#246).
- `-Wconversion -Wsign-conversion` are on, as errors under `Mallard_WARNINGS_AS_ERRORS` (#267, #269–#276).

### Fixed

- `[integrals] budget = true` with `double_flux = true` threw "Double flux needs the cells' thermodynamics frozen" (#252).
- The 3D TENO host retry wrote compacted cells out of bounds (#263).
- Latent truncations of `Solver::get_step()`, mesh local indices and LU substitution indices (#267).
- The radiation test failed to compile with nvcc under `-Werror` (#268).

## [0.7.0] - 2026-10-09

Large-eddy simulation with explicit subgrid-scale and combustion models, a
kinetic-energy-preserving hybrid flux, synthetic turbulent inflow, and 2.2×
faster 3D TENO. First release under Apache-2.0.

### Added

- LES eddy-viscosity models: `[les] model = "sigma" | "wale" | "vreman" | "smagorinsky"`, output `MU_T` (#203); Scotti filter width, the 3D default (#223); opt-in dynamic constant `[les] dynamic = true` (#223).
- Hybrid KEP + Ducros flux: `[numerics] convective_flux = "hybrid"` (#205).
- Kinetic-energy budget: `[integrals] budget = true` (#203).
- Thickened-flame combustion model `[les.combustion] model = "tfles"`, outputs `TF_F`, `TF_E`, `TF_OMEGA` (#215); experimental PaSR (`model = "pasr"`, output `PASR_KAPPA`) (#224); `subgrid_velocity = "eddy_viscosity"` (#222).
- Synthetic turbulent inflow for `nscbc_inlet` through `[boundaries.turbulence]`; inlet `u` accepts expressions (#221).
- 3D single-precision builds pass every test and run in CI (#237).
- Examples `autoignition_2d` (#174), `detonation_3d` and `shock_bubble_3d` (#183), `cbc_les` (#213), `channel_les` (#214, #230), `flame_turbulence` (#222), `channel_inflow_retau180` and `turbulent_inflow_duct` (#221); `mechanisms/he_air.yaml`.

### Changed

- **Licensed under Apache-2.0** (#231), with a `NOTICE` file; 0.1.0–0.6.0 remain AGPL-3.0-only.
- **Example CFLs raised toward the measured stability limits** (#210): most examples run at `cfl = 1.0`; their results are not bitwise equal to 0.6.0.
- **`channel_retau180` uses the hybrid flux** (#216).
- **3D TENO stencils are bounded by a Lebesgue constant of 4** (#232): changes 3D TENO results on tetrahedra, pyramids, mixed meshes and some prisms and hexahedra next to walls; TENO cache format `MALLARD-TENO-8`.
- **SSPRK3's last stage is `U + 2/3 (U2′ − U)`** (#237): results change at round-off; unstretched generated 3D meshes end exactly at `L`.
- **NSCBC transverse terms** use edge neighbors in 3D, skip faces next to non-characteristic boundaries and reversed outlet faces, and continue across periodic seams (#238, #221): 3D NSCBC results change.
- Restart files of runs with characteristic boundaries use format version 4, which 0.6.0 cannot read (#219).
- MallardReactor's ignition criterion is the peak of dT/dt (#220).
- `eps_numerical` is taken from the scheme's discrete pressure work, new column `pressure_work` (#222).
- 3D TENO smooth pass 2.2× faster on GPUs (#212); TENO setup host memory 6× lower on CUDA (#227).

### Fixed

- NSCBC runs restart bitwise (#219).
- `nscbc_outlet` no longer diverges when eddies reverse the flow (#238).
- A turbulent outflow could make the NSCBC outlet pressure drift (#221).
- 3D TENO grew modes next to walls and on jittered meshes (#232).

## [0.6.0] - 2026-10-05

Axisymmetric flows, non-reflecting boundaries, in-situ statistics, parallel
HDF5 output and AMD GPUs.

### Added

- Axisymmetric (r–z) flows: `[physics] axisymmetric = true` in 2D builds (#184).
- Characteristic boundaries `nscbc_outlet` and `nscbc_inlet`, and `[[sponges]]` (#176).
- Roe and RHLL for gas mixtures (#187).
- In-situ statistics `[statistics]` and probes `[[probes]]` (#169); `tools/plane_average.py`.
- Parallel HDF5 output with XDMF: `[[write_data]] format = "hdf5"` (#179).
- Output variables `Q` and `VORTICITY` (#139); `[integrals]` columns `velocity_squared`, `vorticity_squared`, `density_squared`, `temperature`, `temperature_squared` (#170).
- Inflow compositions as expressions of `x`, `y`, `z` (#167); `viscosity_model = "power_law"`, `[source] mass_flow` and `[mesh] stretching` (#170, #172).
- AMD GPUs (Kokkos HIP) (#180).
- `cache_single_precision` for TENO caches (#151).
- GPU performance suite `benchmarks/perf/` and `tools/perf_suite.py` (#168); `tools/animate_cartesian.py` (#178).
- Examples `isotropic_turbulence`, `triple_flame`, `flame_vortex`, `channel_retau180`, `counterflow_diffusion`, `poiseuille_pipe`, `noh_axisymmetric`, `sedov_axisymmetric`, `sphere_axisymmetric` and `flame_outflow`.

### Changed

- **Conventional CFL normalization** (#175): the same `cfl` gives twice the time step as in 0.5.0. **Halve `cfl` in inputs written for 0.5 or earlier to keep their results.** The `CFL` output halves accordingly.
- **3D analytical initial conditions** use a degree-5 tetrahedron rule and `n_subdivisions = 1` by default (#188): much faster, but 3D initial states differ at round-off (smooth fields) or in cells cut by a discontinuity.
- **Double-flux mixtures with HLL, Rusanov and HLLC** take wave speeds from each side's own frozen `(gamma, e0)` (#187).
- TENO-E setup 5.6× faster; results change at round-off (#181).

### Fixed

- Fractional reaction orders below 1 gave NaN Jacobians before ignition (#177).
- Monitor files of a run started from a restart file get their header (#170).
- The single-precision build compiles and passes again (#185, #190).
